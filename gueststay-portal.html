A self-service portal so guests stop doing check-in twice

A new responsive web product where hotel guests pre-check-in, manage their booking, add extras and download invoices. Designed from zero and built on the same system as the kiosk and booking engine.

The problem

The real issue wasn't the form, it was expectations. Guests who did "pre-check-in" online thought they were fully checked in, then got asked again at the hotel. That made the feature feel like extra work instead of a shortcut.

There was also a data constraint. The portal only worked when the hotel had the guest's real email, which OTAs like Booking.com usually withhold. So the portal's reach depended on direct bookings, which shaped how we thought about the whole ecosystem.

What I found

I interviewed hoteliers in the UK and Germany, Guestline's core markets, and ran first-impression tests of the pre-check-in flow with potential guests in Maze.

  • Many guests assumed pre-check-in was the full check-in.
  • Manual data entry caused errors that staff then had to fix.
  • Guests often called the hotel after their stay just to get an invoice.
  • Hotels wanted their own branding and proof of ROI.
  • Competitor portals mostly digitised forms, with little in the way of upsells or customisation.

Key decisions

Make pre-check-in data flow straight into the kiosk

Whatever a guest completes online pre-fills the kiosk, so arrival takes under a minute. This is what makes pre-check-in feel worth doing.

Rewrite the communications, not just the UI

"Check in now!" became "Complete our secure and fast check-in form before you arrive". Honest copy fixed the double check-in complaint more than any screen change.

Put upsells where the guest is already planning

Breakfast, late check-out, parking and upgrades sit inside pre-check-in, when guests are thinking about their stay, not after they've arrived.

Cover the whole stay, including after it

View, edit or cancel a booking with clear policies, and download past invoices, all without contacting staff.

One system, three products

The portal was built on the Guest Design System from day one. It shares components and principles with the kiosk and the booking engine, scales across devices, and lets each hotel bring its own logo, colours and type without breaking usability or accessibility.

What I'd take from it

We launched the portal as its own product, in parallel to the kiosk. Because they share the same design system and backend, we're now moving towards one unified GuestStay experience, so hotels deploy one thing and guests feel one journey.
