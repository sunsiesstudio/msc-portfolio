Modernising a booking engine without breaking what worked

The Direct Booking Manager is the booking engine embedded on hotel websites. It worked, but it looked dated next to OTAs. I redesigned it to build trust, convert on mobile and act as the front door to the rest of GuestStay.

Why it mattered

The booking engine is where the guest journey starts, and it's the one place hotels get a guest's real email and details. OTAs keep that data. Without it, guests never get invited to the portal, so they miss pre-check-in, upsells and invoices. In other words, the whole ecosystem depends on this step converting.

The brief wasn't to rebuild from scratch. It was to evolve a reliable product so it felt as modern and trustworthy as an OTA, native to each hotel's website, and consistent with the kiosk and portal.

What I found

I started with funnel and device data to find where people abandoned, then ran Maze baseline tests and Hotjar analysis, plus hotelier interviews and an OTA benchmark.

  • Guests compared it directly with OTA flows. Mobile-first and fast was the baseline.
  • Mobile flows had clear friction points, and people hesitated around pricing.
  • Hoteliers wanted it to feel like part of their site, not a plug-in.

We set success metrics up front around conversion, drop-offs and data capture.

Key decisions

Evolution, not reinvention

Hotels relied on it daily. I kept the proven flow and focused on hierarchy, typography, responsiveness and clarity, which lowered risk for engineering and for hotels.

Make pricing and policies impossible to miss

Pricing hesitation was a trust problem, so clearer price breakdowns and visible policies were a priority from the first iteration.

Deeper white-label controls

Hotels can set colours, fonts and even border radius to match their site, while accessibility standards stay locked in by the system.

Where it is now

Guestline's acquisition slowed the full rollout, so the live product is currently a mix of old and new. The validated design is ready, and the next step is full adoption across hotels. Tested results came from Maze and Hotjar rather than full live numbers, which is something I'd want to close the loop on.
